CONCEPT 01

Guided Self-Check

A gated checklist that reveals one item at a time. The user actively taps “I have it” for each — a tiny commitment that converts passive reading into recall. “Start” stays locked until all three are confirmed; any “Not now” slides open a warm reschedule offer.

PreparednessRescheduleProgressive disclosure
Interaction
Tap-to-confirm, one card at a time. The active card is highlighted; upcoming cards are dimmed & locked. The primary CTA is disabled with the honest label “Confirm all items to start.”
Animation
Confirmed card collapses to a green ticked row; the next slides up & brightens. Progress dots fill (indigo→green).
Layout
Same rows, same PAN-first order — now stateful. Adds a 3-dot progress bar and an inline reschedule panel.
Preparedness ↑
Active confirmation (“I have it”) forces a real mental check per item vs. skimming a list.
Reschedule ↑
“Not now” is a first-class answer, not a corner link — it opens a positive “hold your slot” path in-context.
Trade-offs
Adds ~3 taps and a gate — motivated, ready users feel mild friction. Mitigate by keeping copy tight and animations <300ms. A/B the hard gate vs. a soft nudge (allow start but warn).

CONCEPT 02

Original vs. Copy — the elimination

A 1.2-second animation that does the teaching. Three tempting shortcuts — photocopy, screenshot, digital image — appear, get an ✗ stamp, desaturate and fall away, while the real card scales up with an indigo glow and a green “this is the one.” It shows what not to bring without a single warning sentence.

Original PANMotion hierarchyMicro-animation
Interaction
Auto-plays once when the screen scrolls into view (and on “Replay”). No taps required — it’s an ambient reveal, so it never blocks the ready user.
Animation
Decoys: ✗ stamp → grayscale → drop & fade. Hero: spring scale-up + indigo ring glow, then the green badge rises in.
Layout
PAN block gets a decoy row above it; title itself carries “original.” Pen/paper & internet demote to compact rows.
Preparedness ↑
Naming the exact wrong cases (copy/screenshot/image) pre-empts the top-3 real failure modes visually.
Reschedule ↑
A calm “Missing your PAN? Reschedule” sits right under Start — targeted at the exact person who just realized they only have a copy.
Trade-offs
Motion is decorative, not a gate — a distracted user can still miss it. Pairs well with Concept 1’s confirm gate. Respect prefers-reduced-motion (falls back to the end-state).

CONCEPT 03

Readiness Cards — swipe to review

The list becomes a short swipeable deck: one requirement per card, PAN first and biggest. Because each item owns the full screen for a moment, it can’t be skimmed past. The final card is a decision, not a button-race — “Start” and “hold my slot” presented as two equal choices.

PreparednessRescheduleSwipe cards
Interaction
Swipe (or arrow/dot tap) through 3 requirement cards → 1 decision card. Progress dots + “1 / 4” keep it short and legible.
Animation
Horizontal track slides with an ease-out spring; dots animate indigo→green; the summary card lands on a soft green field.
Layout
Full-bleed carousel replaces the stacked list. Biggest structural change of the set — an “experimental direction.”
Preparedness ↑
One-item-per-card focus + the “most important” tag on PAN forces attention onto each requirement in turn.
Reschedule ↑
The end-card poses a genuine either/or with equal visual weight, so “hold my slot” reads as a choice, not a retreat.
Trade-offs
Highest interaction cost — the CTA is 3 swipes deep, which can dent start-rate for the already-ready. Consider a “Skip — I’m ready” shortcut on card 1 for repeat/confident users.

CONCEPT 04

Smart Gate — the CTA answers back

Three plain Yes/No questions. The primary button listens: all-yes and it’s a confident “Start Video KYC”; a single “No” and it morphs — indigo and prominent — into “Hold my priority slot,” because for an unprepared user that genuinely is the better button. The system routes people to the right outcome instead of hoping they self-select.

RescheduleOriginal PANAdaptive CTA
Interaction
Tap Yes/No per item. The footer CTA + hint update live. “No” on PAN swaps the primary action to reschedule; the start option demotes to a quiet link.
Animation
Selected segment fills (green Yes / red-text No) with a quick scale-tick; the CTA cross-fades label & color on state change.
Layout
List items rewritten as questions with an inline segmented control. Footer keeps one adaptive primary button.
Preparedness ↑
A direct question (“in hand?”) is harder to skim than a statement; the user has to answer honestly to proceed.
Reschedule ↑
Biggest expected lift. Unready users are actively guided to reschedule as the primary CTA — not left to find a secondary link.
Trade-offs
Users can lie to reach “Start” — but the honest “No→reschedule” path still captures a big share who’d otherwise fail live. Keep the start link reachable so we don’t trap a false-negative. Strongest when combined with Concept 2’s visual for “original.”

CONCEPT 05

Ambient PAN Spotlight — zero friction

The lightest-touch option, closest to today’s layout. A soft breathing halo draws the eye to the PAN card, a persistent “ORIGINAL” badge sits on it, and one line below quietly cycles the four cases — not a photocopy → not a screenshot → not a digital image → the original card only. Nothing to tap; the message lands while the user’s thumb hovers over Start.

Original PANAmbient motionMinimal change
Interaction
None required — purely ambient. It’s the safe, low-risk baseline change and the easiest to ship / A/B against the current screen.
Animation
Gentle radial halo pulse (2.6s loop) + a text cycler that fades each “not a…” line up through to “original card only.”
Layout
Near-identical to today. Adds the halo, an ORIGINAL badge, one cycling caption line, and upgrades reschedule to an outlined button.
Preparedness ↑
Motion + the rotating exclusions make the “original only” rule salient without adding a step. Modest but real lift.
Reschedule ↑
“Missing your PAN? Reschedule” as an outlined button (vs. faint text link) gives it real presence beside Start.
Trade-offs
No forced attention — the fully checked-out user can still ignore it, so expect a smaller preparedness gain than the interactive concepts. Best as a low-risk first ship or a control-adjacent variant.

CONCEPT 06

Reschedule-Forward — reframe the choice

Instead of Start (big) vs. Schedule (small escape), the screen opens with an even question: “When would you like to verify?” — “I’m ready now” or “In a few minutes.” Reschedule stops being a failure exit and becomes a legitimate, low-guilt option chosen up front. Picking “In a few minutes” reframes the whole panel around holding a priority slot.

RescheduleFramingCTA hierarchy
Interaction
A two-option segmented control at the top sets intent. It swaps the panel content and the footer CTA between “Start Video KYC” and “Hold my priority slot.”
Animation
Segment thumb slides; panel cross-fades between an indigo “ready” checklist and a green “we’ll hold your slot” reassurance card; CTA label/colour transitions.
Layout
Reschedule is promoted from a secondary button to half of the primary decision. Single adaptive footer CTA instead of two stacked buttons.
Preparedness ↑
Framing the moment as “when,” with the requirement line right under it, prompts a readiness self-assessment before committing.
Reschedule ↑
Directly targets the 6.7% problem: equal placement + positive “few minutes / priority slot” language removes the stigma of the escape hatch.
Trade-offs
Making reschedule this prominent could pull some ready users into deferring — watch that total completions don’t drop. Keep “I’m ready now” pre-selected (default bias toward starting) and measure downstream completion of rescheduled sessions.

How they fit together

These aren’t six competing skins — they’re a ladder of interaction cost. Ship Concept 5 (Ambient Spotlight) first: it’s the lowest-risk change and a clean A/B against today. Run Concept 4 (Smart Gate) and Concept 1 (Guided Self-Check) as the preparedness bets — they force an honest check and route unready users to reschedule. Fold Concept 2’s original-vs-copy animation into whichever wins, since it teaches the #1 failure with zero added steps. Concept 6 is the reschedule-conversion experiment; Concept 3 is the experimental full-redesign to hold in reserve.
